TPRM Must Change for the AI Era
By Amiel Attias
In Brief

For decades, Third-Party Risk Management assumed that trusting the vendor reduced the risk. AI breaks that assumption: the same platform can be deployed to summarize public content or connected to proprietary code and privileged systems — same vendor, radically different risk. The implementation, not the vendor, is now the real unit of trust. This piece introduces the Contextual Trust Framework, five questions that replace a single vendor risk score, and argues that trust must be evaluated continuously rather than assessed once and left alone.

The Business Problem Isn't AI

Security was built to move in weeks. AI moves in days.

Every executive faces the same tension: the business wants AI deployed in days, while security was designed to evaluate technology over weeks. That gap is quickly becoming one of the biggest operational risks facing modern enterprises.

The challenge isn't simply approving AI faster. It's enabling innovation without losing confidence in how technology is adopted. That means changing the question itself — not "Is this vendor secure?" but "Can we trust this implementation?" That is a fundamentally different question, and it demands a fundamentally different operating model.

The Unit of Trust Has Changed

Traditional TPRM treats the vendor as the unit of analysis. That no longer reflects how organizations consume AI.

Risk is no longer determined primarily by who provides the technology. It emerges from the relationship between the technology and the business using it. The same vendor may represent minimal risk in one implementation and critical risk in another — not because the vendor changed, but because everything around it did.

The implementation becomes the unit of trust.

The Contextual Trust Framework

Five dimensions determine Implementation Trust

There is no universal vendor risk score. There is only implementation-specific trust — built from five questions, not one.

Vendor
Who provides the technology?
Business Context
Why is it being used?
Data Sensitivity
What information can it access?
Granted Capabilities
What actions can it perform?
Required Security Controls
Which controls are necessary for this implementation?

Together, these dimensions replace the vendor risk score. The same vendor can represent minimal risk in one implementation and critical risk in another — not because the vendor changed, but because everything around it did.

Why Traditional TPRM Doesn't Scale

Trust can no longer be a point-in-time decision

The challenge isn't a shortage of talented analysts. It's that the operating model assumes trust can be assessed periodically — and AI changes too quickly for that assumption to hold.

Business priorities evolve. Implementations expand. Permissions accumulate. Models gain new capabilities. Data flows change.

Adding more analysts simply scales yesterday's operating model. It does not solve tomorrow's problem.
From Vendor Assessment to Context Assessment

This is where AI can redesign TPRM itself

Not because AI completes questionnaires faster — but because it can continuously understand context. It can correlate evidence, monitor implementation changes, identify control gaps, recognize shifts in business usage, and surface emerging risks.

Note

AI enables organizations to evaluate trust continuously, not periodically. That's a more meaningful transformation than simply automating assessments.

Where Human Judgment Matters Most

None of this reduces the importance of security professionals. It increases it.

Machines are exceptionally good at gathering evidence, detecting change, correlating information, and identifying inconsistencies. Humans remain responsible for what matters most: making trade-offs, balancing business objectives against security risk, designing compensating controls, accepting risk, and building organizational trust.

The role of the TPRM professional evolves from evidence collection to strategic decision-making.
Strategic Implications — Continuous Trust

Two questions replace the annual assessment

Future operating models should continuously answer two simple questions: Has the vendor changed? Has our implementation changed? Either answer may change the organization's risk.

Trust is no longer static. Neither is TPRM.

Practical Recommendations — The Real Transformation

Don't automate yesterday's process. Redesign the operating model.

For more than two decades, TPRM has treated the vendor as the unit of trust. Today, the implementation becomes the unit of trust — and that single shift changes how we assess technology, design security controls, prioritize risk, allocate security resources, and ultimately, enable innovation.

Because in the AI era, the question is no longer "Can we trust this vendor?" It is "Can we trust this implementation?"

Questions for Leaders

Sit with these before your next vendor review

  1. When did we last evaluate an AI implementation rather than the vendor behind it?
  2. Do we know what data, capabilities, and controls are actually present in each deployment — today, not at approval time?
  3. Is our TPRM operating model built for a point-in-time decision, or a continuously changing condition?
